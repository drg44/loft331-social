# Wink humour — what actually lands (researched 2026-09-21 after Dave rejected 6 winks in a row)

Dave's verdict on my own jokes: "not how people talk", "I don't get it", "not coherent".
Root cause: I was inventing premises. Proven WFH humour reuses a handful of premises EVERYONE has lived,
stated plainly, and lets the reader supply the laugh. Never invent a premise. Adapt a proven one.

## Proven premises (each one has been shared millions of times; verified in Reader's Digest / HuffPost roundups)
1. Cat/dog joins the call.   "Me: I have a Zoom meeting later. My cat: Oh, me too."
2. On mute / not on mute.    "Not muting your mic is the new reply-all."
3. Waking up right before the call. "Wakes up 4 minutes before my first call."
4. Pants / dress code.       "Yoga pants or sweatpants?"  "Hours are made up and your pants don't matter."
5. Kitchen drift.            "Anyone else keep finding themselves in the kitchen with no idea how they got there?"
6. Meetings that should be emails. "Finding out which meetings could've been emails after all."
7. Losing track of days / talking to objects. "Day 8: engages in conversation with a lamp."
8. Partner's work voice.     "I'm married to a 'let's circle back' guy — who knew?"
9. Work interfering with WFH. "Work is really interfering with my enjoyment of working from home."
10. Pets as coworkers.       "Every day is bring-your-pet-to-work day."

## Forms that work
- **Dialogue:** "Me: … / My cat: …"   (two lines = two speakers; instantly readable)
- **Plain statement of the shared truth**, no twist needed: "Woke up four minutes before my first call."
- **Redefinition:** "X is the new Y."
- **Understatement** in the subline, where Loft 331 shows up as the quiet fix. The subline never explains the joke.

## Forms that fail (Dave, 2026-09-21)
- Invented scenarios nobody has lived ("the cat runs the meetings", "reheated three times, it's soup").
- Two abstract nouns joined by a colon ("Coffee-shop Wi-Fi: two bars and a prayer").
- Anything that needs a second read, or a subline to explain it.
- Poetic fragments ("Morning light. Quiet desks.").

## The test (all three, out loud, before rendering)
1. Is this premise on the list above, or something Dave has personally said?
2. Would a Moncton realtor say this sentence to a friend, word for word?
3. Does the photo literally show it (cat, mute icon, pyjama pants, kitchen)?
If any answer is no → don't render. Show Dave three options instead.
