## 2. Rules for a wink (each cited)

1. **The joke is a true fact, stated flat.** Vikki Ross (D&AD): "what's the fact?" No Name: "will not replace oven". Deadpan Cards: "if you can't say something nice, say something factually accurate."
2. **Office vocabulary on the domestic.** The single biggest signal in the viral set: the cat is a "coworker", plants get "performance reviews", "On my way to working from home", the imaginary colleague "Cheryl" (682k likes). The humour is the mismatch of register, not a punchline.
3. **Hyper-specific detail.** "grey sweats on grey sweats", "macbook air on the couch, cup of tea", "three-minute bursts in a communal bathroom". BuzzFeed literally titles its list "Hilariously *Specific* Tweets". Harry Dry: "Can I visualize the words? Can I falsify the words?"
4. **First-person confession, no "confession:" label.** "I've never written 'Hope this email finds you well' and meant it."
5. **Reported speech from a bystander.** Kids on calls: "FBI - open up!!!"; the partner who turned out to be a "let's circle back" guy. Transcribe, don't comment.
6. **Corporate-to-plain translation.** "Moving forward = Don't try me again"; "per my last email". Decoding workplace phrases is the dominant premise in every email roundup found.
7. **Leave the punchline to the reader.** V&A on The Economist's "Management trainee. Aged 42.": "eight words and a number… provoke a double take." Avis never names Hertz.
8. **Trim, then trim again.** Dave Harland: "Trim your jokes. Then trim them again. And again." Harry Dry: ready "when there's nothing left to remove."
9. **Keep a straight face.** Mailchimp: "forced humor can be worse than none at all… prefer winking to shouting." No emoji, no subline explaining it, no "see what we did there".
10. **Joke about the audience's problem, never the audience.** Sprout 2017: 88% find brands making fun of consumers "annoying"; snark wanted by one third. Harland: "jokes about the problems you solve for your audience."
11. **Own a small true weakness, not a big one.** VW "Lemon.", Avis "No. 2". For Loft 331: the weather, Moncton, the lift. Never members, never price.
12. **Maritime register, not Newfoundland.** Halifax Live: "Maritime humor values understatement and gentle teasing over brash sarcasm… dry, delivered with a side of self-mockery." Salon: Canadian humour's "central concern… is self-deprecation." Weather, Tims and "sorry" are legitimate premises only as specific observation, never as an "eh" stereotype.
13. **Set the ceiling once.** Who Gives A Crap's CEO: "We'll never say something that's dirtier than our brand name." Loft 331 floor: *nothing we wouldn't say to a member's face in the kitchen.*

**What not to do (sourced flops):** puns and wordplay (only on auto-generated pun farms, never in curated roundups; Ogilvy "had little time for jokes, especially puns"); setup-then-punchline joke form; "X is the new Y"; the 3 PM slump; dated pandemic framing (mine the 2020 lines for *structure*, don't repost Zoom/quarantine specifics); slang to look cool (Sprout 2017); a line whose punchline is a picture; trend-chasing (JMR 2025: cringe "occurs when brands make a failed attempt… to appear relevant, humorous"; and "cringe is highly shareable").

## 4. Rules for a Thursday question (each cited)

1. **Answerable in five seconds**: two options, one blank, or one word. Fill-in-the-blank had "significantly more comments than any other type" (Simply Measured); questions double comments at the cost of likes/shares (Sotrender 10k posts; HubSpot/Zarrella).
2. **Specific and lived, never generic.** "What is your first win of the year?" beats "Any resolutions?"; Boomp: "Coffee or tea?… feel[s] empty" for local businesses; AuthoredUp: LinkedIn now penalises "lazy polls", naming "Coffee or tea?".
3. **Should / would / who / which outperform why / how** (HubSpot data).
4. **No obvious right answer; neutral; no religion or politics.** JustPollen: "the best ones hit a topic where reasonable people disagree."
5. **Ask, don't beg.** Meta demotes "Comment below / Tag 3 friends / Like if" as engagement bait (transparency.meta.com); genuine questions and "help, advice or recommendations" asks are exempt.
6. **Local and seasonal.** Weather, storm chips, snow days, patio season, the Loft itself (HeyOrca, St. John's; Post Planner: tie to "seasons and weather"). Name the holiday (Veterans Affairs' "March holiday season" post drew 4,800+ angry comments).
7. **Question last in the caption and on the image; reply within the hour** (Smash Balloon; Hootsuite 2.4x; LinkedIn 30–60 min).
8. **Judge on replies, not reach.** Comments are not a top Instagram ranking signal (Mosseri, Jan 2025); the slot exists to produce replies and future posts (Buffer, Sprout).
9. **Remembrance week is the exception.** No fun question, no poppy graphic, no offer. See §6.

## 6. Holiday tone rules (Canada / New Brunswick)

**Remembrance Day (Nov 11).** A commemoration, not a greeting.
- DO: "Lest we forget" as the whole message; the 11:00 a.m. two minutes of silence; a local ceremony time; a photo of a real worn poppy if any visual at all. Keep it plain and short (Business Ethics Blog: "craft its message carefully"; some say businesses shouldn't post at all).
- DON'T: put a poppy in any branded graphic, promotion or signage (Legion trademark: "can never be used… for commercial… display… without the express written permission"; the Legion removed 12,310 Amazon listings in 2024). No deal, special or price near the day (Gabbo's nightclub, Calgary realtor, Maxwell's Plum, The Gap all retracted and apologised). Never "Happy Remembrance Day". Never a brand campaign (BMO #TakePause backlash). Never "long weekend".
- Loft 331: the Nov 11 morning post is "Lest we forget" only, dark gradient, no poppy in the template, no sell. If Loft & Found falls on Nov 11 (2026), its posts that week carry no "party" language. The Thursday question that week is skipped or reflective.

**Canada Day:** respectful, light; celebratory and reflective split evenly in polling.
**Good Friday / Easter, Christmas, Thanksgiving:** name the holiday (Veterans Affairs "March holiday season" backlash).
**No Halloween content** (Dave, 2026-10-14).
**Theming starts one to two weeks before each holiday**, not on the day (Dave, 2026-10-14).

## 7. Brand craft rules that carry over (pass 3)

- Two beats, long then short, both true: "When you're only No. 2, you try harder. Or else." / "Don't be scared. It's just water." Maps onto the Fraunces two-line headline.
- Say who it's not for, gently: "Management trainee. Aged 42."; coworking slogans "Work independently, not alone." and "Because working alone sucks." (forum.coworking.org). The alternative is the kitchen table, the café with one plug, never a person.
- The No Name test, not the Ryanair test: Canadian professional audiences reward flat, true description ("will not replace oven", "80 sheets not mountains"), not clapbacks. Ryanair's jokes backfired whenever the brand owed people something.
- Small brands get latitude (Mediacat: "smaller brands… can be funny"); 91% prefer funny brands (Oracle 2022) but 77% of under-28s say brands "can seem like older people attempting humor with them" (Omnicom/Snapchat, 6,000 respondents incl. Canada).
- Never let the joke get in the way of the functional line (Spotler; Mailchimp: clarity outranks entertainment).

## 8. How winks and questions get made from now on

1. **Winks come from Dave's Winks tab first.** If it's empty, Claude does not write one.
2. **If Dave wants options**, Claude offers three, each a verbatim or minimally trimmed line from §3 (or a newly sourced one with its URL), labelled with premise and source. Dave picks or rejects. Nothing renders without a pick.
3. **Every option passes the five tests before it's offered:** it's a true fact stated flat (rule 1); it uses one of the viral structures (rules 2–6); it fits in two lines of 26 without being copy-edited into "brand voice" (rule 8); it has no pun, no "X is the new Y", no setup/punchline (what-not-to-do list); the photo literally shows it.
4. **Questions** are built only from §5 formats and examples, localized (Moncton, the season, the Loft), with the source noted in the calendar's Content Theme column.
5. **Holiday calendar check** before every cycle (§6), themed one to two weeks ahead.
